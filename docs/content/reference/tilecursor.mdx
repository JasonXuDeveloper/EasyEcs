A tile's texture at the first cell of a row ([`Tile.At`](/en/docs/reference/tile#at)): the cells around it, one or a vector at a time. An offset is in cells: dx from −r (the first halo cell before the row) to Width + r − 1 (the last after it), dy and dz from −r to r (dy 0 for a 2D tile), r the shape's [`ITileShape.Halo`](/en/docs/reference/itileshape#halo); a vector of cells must end by the last halo cell after the row. Constant offsets are checked by the JIT, others by a compare.

#### Type parameters

- `TCell`: The cell.
- `TTile`: The tile's component and shape.

## Methods

### `Columns` [#columns]

```csharp
public TileColumns<TCell, TTile> Columns(int step);
```

The columns of the row from 0, every `step` cells while under Width, walked with `foreach` (`foreach (var col in c.Columns(4))`, a vector of four floats per column): each a cursor at its column, whose loads take offsets from that column and whose `col.Store(vector, into)` writes the row the step writes at the column. The walk knows its first and last column, so a load at constant offsets is checked by the JIT alone, a store by one compare of the row's length, and the loads of one column share one address with each offset folded into the instruction: the loop a stencil would write by hand over its own texture. O(1) per column; throws when `step` is not 1 to Width.
